    How the numbers are produced

    • Model. Air is treated as an ideal gas at constant temperature (Boyle's law): bottle content is proportional to volume × absolute pressure. Connecting a donor to a recipient equalises both to the volume-weighted mean pressure P = (VdPd + VrPr) / (Vd + Vr). The gauge/absolute offset cancels in that expression, so all pressures you enter and read are gauge pressures.
    • Real fills run warm. Gas heats on compression and cools in the donor, so a bottle settled back to ambient temperature reads a little lower than shown here. Treat the results as the isothermal best case.
    • Smart vs. dumb. Smart connects the lowest-pressure usable donor first and keeps the highest-pressure bottle in reserve for the final top-up, which extracts far more usable gas from the same bank. Dumb equalises the fullest donor first and works down.
    • Duty cycle. A fill that reaches the counts as a fill threshold — by default half of the bottle's working range between minimum and maximum — is counted; the bottle then goes into service, comes back at its minimum pressure and queues for a refill. The first fill that falls short of the threshold is still shown, with its resulting pressure, but ends that bottle's campaign because every later attempt would deliver less. Raise the threshold if only near-full bottles are useful to you, lower it to squeeze the bank dry.
    • Safety limit. A donor above a recipient's maximum working pressure is never connected unless Allow unsafe filling is on; those transfers are then flagged. Transfers always stop at the recipient's maximum, the surplus stays in the donor.

    Planning aid only. Verify every pressure against the bottle and valve markings and the rules that apply to you before filling anything.
